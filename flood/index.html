03 / Why hyperlocal failures happen

Same storm. Different street.

A useful local system needs to represent both fast-changing rainfall and the slower, physical paths water takes through a city.

01

Weather has its own chaos

Convective rain can vary sharply across short distances and time windows. A citywide alert can miss a neighborhood-scale burst, even when the broad storm signal is right.

Weather uncertainty is distinct from whether a particular drain or underpass can pass the water.

02

Water follows a graph

Streets, culverts, nalas and basins route runoff across administrative boundaries. A routed catchment graph can connect upstream rainfall with downstream low points.

Terrain and network connectivity help describe where water may gather; they do not guarantee a working drainage asset.

03

Inflow meets outflow

Surface runoff adds inflow. Drainage and channel conveyance provide outflow. Short-lived mismatch can create local pressure, shaped by blockages, storage and boundary conditions.

This demo compresses that complex process into a transparent ratio to make assumptions inspectable.

04 / Sensors and sparse places

Ungauged does not mean unknowable.

It means the uncertainty must be visible, and any transfer from observed places must earn its way through validation.

A

Let sparse data widen the interval

When upstream gauges are missing, rainfall and flow context are less constrained. A responsible output should widen its uncertainty range and say what is absent.

B

Borrow by similarity

Nearby catchments may share slope, land cover, drainage form or storm exposure. Similarity transfer can be a starting hypothesis, with distance and mismatch recorded.

C

Keep sensors in the loop

Rain gauges, water-level sensors and field reports can correct model assumptions. Their coverage, latency and maintenance need explicit tracking.

05 / Roadmap and validation

Earn trust in stages.

Move from a transparent prototype to a decision aid only through reproducible, time-aware evaluation with local partners.

01 · Build

Assemble a routed catchment representation from terrain, lake and nala layers; document coverage and known topology gaps.

02 · Label

Create time-stamped historical flood labels from verified reports and infrastructure records. Preserve missingness and location uncertainty.

03 · Hold out time

Train on earlier periods and evaluate later storms. Avoid leakage between nearby events; report performance by neighborhood and event.

04 · Compare

Compare with the IMD alert baseline. Measure false alarms, missed events, lead time, calibration and performance under sensor gaps.

05 · Review

Review outputs with city engineers and affected communities before considering any operational use. This page does not provide that use.
